Basics

Free settings anyone can change.

Check your battery health first

Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging

Maximum Capacity shows how much of the original capacity is left. If it has fallen well below 80%, no setting fully fixes it, and a battery replacement is the real solution.

Find what's draining your battery

Settings > Battery

Apple's battery screen shows each app's share of usage, with labels that explain why: Background Activity, Location, Home & Lock Screen (the display woken by notifications), and No Cell Coverage / Low Signal. Then fix the biggest offenders: limit Background App Refresh or Location for that app, turn off its notifications, or use Airplane Mode in low signal. Usage isn't shown while the phone is charging.

Use Dark Mode, dark apps and a mostly black wallpaper

Settings > Appearance > Dark (or Settings > Display & Brightness > Dark)

The 13 mini has an OLED display, where black pixels use almost no power. Pick a wallpaper that is mostly pure black (#000000) with some detail, and switch on dark or OLED themes in the apps that offer them. True black saves more than dark grey, which still lights the pixels. It only helps while the screen is on. A Purdue study of Android OLED phones found Dark Mode saved only 3% to 9% of power at 30% to 50% brightness, but 39% to 47% at full brightness, so keeping brightness low matters more.

Cut back on 5G (LTE or 5G Auto)

Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data Options > Voice & Data

5G uses more power than LTE. In a Wall Street Journal test, an iPhone 13 mini lasted about 1.5 hours longer on LTE than on 5G while streaming video. 5G Auto trims some of that cost by using 5G only when it's worth it, but it still uses 5G part of the time, so LTE should save slightly more if you don't need 5G speeds. I couldn't find a direct 5G Auto versus LTE test, so the gap is probably smaller than the 5G versus LTE gap. Pick LTE if you never miss the speed, or 5G Auto if your LTE is weak or slow.

Tighten Location Services

Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services

Set apps to While Using or Never unless they truly need background access, like navigation. Turn off Precise Location for apps that only need your general area. You can also scroll to the bottom and tap Location Services > System Services to turn off unneeded background location reporting like Routing & Traffic and iPhone Analytics (leave Find My iPhone and Emergency Calls & SOS on).

Other quick wins

  • Auto-Brightness (Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Auto-Brightness) is a sensible default. Setting brightness low by hand can save more if you keep it low, since the screen is one of the biggest power draws. Lowering brightness is the biggest display lever: the same study found that going from 100% to 50% cuts the display's own power draw roughly tenfold.
  • Turn off Raise to Wake (Settings > Display & Brightness > Raise to Wake) if the screen lights up when you don't need it.
  • Trim notifications. Each one wakes the screen.
  • Prefer Wi-Fi over cellular when it's available.
  • Shorten Auto-Lock (Settings > Display & Brightness > Auto-Lock) to 30 seconds or 1 minute so the screen doesn't stay on after you put the phone down.
  • Keep iOS updated (Settings > General > Software Update). Apple says updates often include energy-saving technologies.
  • Turn off keyboard haptics (Settings > Sounds & Haptics > Keyboard Feedback > Haptic). Apple says they may affect battery life, though not by how much.
  • Don't force-close apps in the App Switcher. Apple's documentation and software chief Craig Federighi confirm that backgrounded apps are frozen in RAM and draw no power; swiping them away forces the CPU to reload them from scratch next time, which uses more energy. Only force-close an app if it's unresponsive.

Make Low Power Mode a habit

Low Power Mode cuts background activity such as mail fetching and background refresh, trims some visual effects and shortens Auto-Lock to 30 seconds. Some people keep it on all day, and one user reported still having about 50% left by 6pm with it always on. The trade-off is slower mail and delayed background updates.

  • For one-tap control, add Low Power Mode to Control Center (Settings > Control Center). Some people also put a Shortcuts toggle on the Lock Screen.
  • iOS turns it off by itself after charging past about 80%, so keeping it on all day needs an automation (see Advanced).

Charging habits

Slowing down battery aging.

Leave Optimized Battery Charging on

Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging

It's on by default. It learns your routine and reduces the time the phone sits fully charged, while aiming for 100% when you usually unplug.

The 13 mini has no true charge limit

The hard 80% Charge Limit exists only on iPhone 15 and newer. If your 13 mini says it's charging to 80%, that's Optimized Battery Charging pausing there and finishing to 100% later. It doesn't stop permanently.

Optional: unplug around 80%

Lower charge levels put less stress on a lithium-ion cell, so this may reduce wear. The trade-off is less battery on hand, and how much it helps in practice is debated. In one MacRumors long-term test, a phone kept at an 80% limit held 94% capacity after 299 cycles, versus roughly 87% to 90% for phones without a limit. That's a small difference from one person's test. Treat it as an optional habit.

Keep it cool

Heat is the biggest enemy of the battery.

  • Don't charge under a pillow, in a hot car, or in direct sun.
  • Take the case off if the phone feels warm while charging.
  • Apple's comfort zone is 16° to 22° C (62° to 72° F). Above 35° C (95° F) can permanently reduce battery capacity, while cold only reduces battery life temporarily.

Consider slower charging

Faster chargers make more heat, and heat ages the battery. A slow 5W charger for routine or overnight charging keeps the phone cooler. Keep a faster one for days you need a quick top-up.

Wired charging runs cooler than wireless

Wireless charging, MagSafe included, loses more energy as heat than a cable does. A "wired MagSafe" doesn't exist: magnetic battery packs and battery cases still charge the phone wirelessly. For long days out or CarPlay in the car, a cable is the cooler option. A MagSafe battery pack or battery case is more convenient and still helps, but expect more warmth.

Avoid draining to 0%

Regularly running the battery flat is harder on it than shallower cycles. Topping up during the day is fine. If you're storing a phone for months, Apple recommends leaving it at about 50% charge, powered off, in a cool place.

Advanced

Background tuning and automations.

Reduce background activity

Apps that refresh in the background wake the phone and use power.

  • Settings > General > Background App Refresh: turn it off for apps that don't need it, or limit it to Wi-Fi. Some people turn it off for everything and say the phone works about the same, with apps updating only when opened.
  • Settings > Mail > Mail Accounts > Fetch New Data: Push can be efficient where supported. For Fetch accounts, a longer interval or Manually uses the least power, with delayed mail as the cost.

Use Low Data Mode on cellular (or pause cellular photo sync)

Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data Options > Data Mode > Low Data Mode

According to Apple's documentation, turning on Low Data Mode automatically disables Background App Refresh, stops automatic downloads and video autoplay, and pauses iCloud Photos updates and automatic backups while on cellular. If you only want to stop newly taken photos and 4K videos from immediately uploading over 5G/LTE, you can turn off Settings > Apps > Photos > Cellular Data instead so uploads wait for Wi-Fi.

Use a Safari content blocker

Settings > Apps > Safari > Extensions

Mobile ads, trackers and auto-playing video scripts keep the CPU and radio active while browsing. Native Safari content blockers pass rules to WebKit ahead of time instead of running continuously in the background. In an automated 2-hour Safari browsing test by Wirecutter, an iPhone used 18% of its battery without an ad blocker and 9% with one enabled.

Automate Low Power Mode with Shortcuts

Shortcuts has no true "phone is idle" trigger. Use one of these instead: Sleep Focus, Time of Day, Battery Level (for example, below 30%), or Charger.

  1. Open Shortcuts > Automation > New Automation.
  2. Choose a trigger.
  3. Add the action Set Low Power Mode > On.
  4. Choose Run Immediately so it doesn't ask each time.
  • To keep Low Power Mode on most of the day, pair a Battery Level or Charger-disconnect trigger with Set Low Power Mode > On, so it comes back after charging.
  • Battery alerts: a Battery Level trigger plus Show Notification can warn you at 40%, 30%, 20% and 10%. That doesn't save battery by itself, but it reminds you to act.

iOS turns Low Power Mode off by itself once the phone charges past about 80%, so charging can override the automation.

Reduce White Point for an extra-dim screen

Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Reduce White Point

It reduces the intensity of bright colors, so the screen can go dimmer than the normal minimum brightness. On an OLED a dimmer screen should draw less power, though the exact savings vary. The trade-off is that it's hard to read in sunlight, and a high value combined with minimum brightness can leave the screen too dark to read.

For quick toggling, add it under Settings > Accessibility > Accessibility Shortcut, then triple-click the side button. One user runs it at 60%.

Night and morning automations

Some people schedule a bedtime automation that turns off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth and turns on Low Power Mode and Airplane Mode, then reverses it at wake-up time (for example, Wi-Fi on and mobile data off at a workplace with good Wi-Fi and poor signal).

  • Phones already use little power overnight, so the extra saving over Low Power Mode alone may be small.
  • Airplane Mode means no calls, texts or emergency alerts overnight. Alarms still work.
  • Check that Shortcuts offers each action you want (Set Airplane Mode, Set Wi-Fi, Set Bluetooth, Set Cellular Data, Set Low Power Mode). A Location Services toggle may not exist.
  • Use a Time of Day trigger and choose Run Immediately.

Fight weak signal

A weak signal makes the cellular radio work harder and drains the battery. Apple recommends Airplane Mode in poor-coverage areas. Some other options:

  • Wi-Fi Calling (Settings > Phone > Wi-Fi Calling), if your carrier supports it, moves calls onto Wi-Fi where cellular coverage is poor.
  • Wi-Fi-based automation: a Shortcuts Wi-Fi trigger can switch Airplane Mode on when you join a known network (home or work, say) and off when you leave. Turn Wi-Fi back on in the same automation, since Airplane Mode switches it off.
  • The catch: in Airplane Mode, normal calls and texts won't reach you over cellular. Wi-Fi Calling and iMessage may still work, depending on your carrier and setup.

Download offline maps before a trip

Maps > your profile picture > Offline Maps > Download New Map

Download over Wi-Fi first. It helps most in areas with weak signal, where the cellular radio works hard. Navigation still uses GPS and the screen, which are the main drains, so the savings are modest. Maps take up storage.

Small or unproven effects

  • Liquid Glass, Reduce Transparency & Reduce Motion (Settings > Appearance > Liquid Glass and Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Reduce Transparency): iOS 27 added a slider to make Liquid Glass more opaque. While many claim toning down Liquid Glass or enabling Reduce Transparency and Reduce Motion saves battery, a controlled 2.5-hour MacRumors test comparing Clear, Tinted, and Tinted with Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and Reduce Motion found all four runs finished within 1 percentage point of each other. Treat these as readability and comfort settings rather than proven battery savers.
  • Connectivity Assist (Settings > Wi-Fi > Connectivity Assist at the bottom, or per network via the (i) button; it was called Wi-Fi Assist under Cellular on iOS 26 and earlier) uses cellular data alongside Wi-Fi when Wi-Fi is slow or unusable. It's on by default if Wi-Fi Assist was on before you updated. Apple says it can use more cellular data, and some users report better battery life with it off, but I found no controlled measurements. Worth a try if your home Wi-Fi is weak, not proven.
  • Widgets & Live Activities: Home screen widgets get a limited refresh budget from iOS, so removing them saves little. Location-based widgets and frequent Lock Screen Live Activities wake the screen and radios more often.
Doesn't apply to the 13 mini: the built-in 80% Charge Limit slider (iPhone 15 and newer only) and Adaptive Power (introduced in iOS 26, needs iPhone 15 Pro or newer).
